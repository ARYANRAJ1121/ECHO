01 — The Problem

When Algorithms Learn to Fix Prices

Imagine five petrol pumps on a highway. Normally, they compete — Pump A charges ₹96, Pump B drops to ₹94, Pump C undercuts to ₹92. This is great for drivers. Economics calls this the Nash Equilibrium.

Now imagine all five owners meet secretly and agree: "Let's all charge ₹120. No undercutting." This is a cartel — it's illegal because consumers have no choice but to overpay. Antitrust authorities like the CCI and DOJ actively hunt for this.

But here's the twist that keeps regulators awake at night: AI does it without the secret room.

Today, companies like Amazon, Uber, and airlines use AI algorithms that automatically adjust prices millions of times per day. These algorithms independently learn that keeping prices high is more profitable than competing.

No human conspired. No emails were exchanged. No meeting took place. Yet prices end up well above the competitive level — the exact outcome antitrust law was designed to prevent.

⚖️
DOJ v. RealPage (2024) — The Department of Justice sued RealPage for using AI to coordinate rent prices across competing landlords, affecting millions of renters nationwide.
02 — The Framework

What ECHO Does

ECHO creates a controlled laboratory where AI agents compete in a simulated market — and we watch whether they learn to collude.

01

Simulate

Five autonomous AI agents set prices simultaneously in a repeated Bertrand oligopoly. Each agent independently maximizes its own profit — no coordination instructions.

02

Detect

A six-method detection pipeline monitors for collusion: Lambda index tracking, NLP clustering, sentiment analysis, ML strategy classification, price forecasting, and causal demand shocks.

03

Analyze

Compare outcomes across agent architectures (LLM, DQN, Q-Learning, Heuristic), validate against real-world market data (US gasoline, Amazon pricing), and extract causal evidence of coordination.

03 — Methodology

Market Model & Agent Design

Multinomial Logit Demand

Consumer choice follows a discrete choice model where market share depends on price, quality, and a taste heterogeneity parameter (μ).

sᵢ(p) = exp((aᵢ − pᵢ) / μ) / Σⱼ exp((aⱼ − pⱼ) / μ)

Collusion Index (Lambda)

Measures how far prices deviate from competitive Nash equilibrium toward joint monopoly pricing. λ = 0 means full competition, λ = 1 means full cartel.

λ = (p̄ − p_Nash) / (p_Monopoly − p_Nash)

04 — Key Findings

05 — Detection Pipeline

Six Independent Evidence Streams

No single method proves collusion. The combination of statistical anomaly, NLP clustering, and causal shocks provides the evidence pipeline required by regulators.

01

Lambda Monitor

Continuous tracking of the collusion index against Nash and monopoly benchmarks with 3-tier severity alerts.

02

NLP Clustering

Embedding-based cosine similarity across agent scratchpads to detect convergent reasoning patterns.

03

Sentiment Analysis

Cooperative, competitive, and predatory intent scoring with drift detection across simulation rounds.

04

Strategy Classifier

Random Forest model classifying agent behavior from 9 engineered features per firm per round.

05

Price Forecaster

Linear regression with lagged features and momentum for early-warning detection of price convergence.

06

Demand Shocks

Exogenous quality perturbation to one firm — if other firms react, that's causal evidence of coordination.
